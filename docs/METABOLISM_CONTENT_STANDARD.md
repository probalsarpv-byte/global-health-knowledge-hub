# Human Metabolism Curriculum Content Standard & Validation Protocol

**Document Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Authoritative Standard  
**Subject:** Human Metabolism (`subject.metabolism`)  
**Target Platform:** Global Health Knowledge Hub  

---

## 1. Educational Mission & Scope

The Human Metabolism curriculum provides a rigorous, accessible, and comprehensive bilingual (English and Bengali) academic foundation in human cellular bioenergetics, intermediate metabolism, enzyme kinetics and regulation, organ fuel coordination, and metabolic medicine.

Every chapter must meet the highest standards of biochemical accuracy, educational pedagogy, clinical relevance, and accessibility.

---

## 2. Three-Tier Content Depth Architecture

Every chapter must implement three distinct depth tiers:

### 2.1 Foundation Tier (`foundation`)
- **Target Audience:** First-time learners, allied health students, introductory biochemistry and biology students.
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Intuitive conceptual orientation, big-picture cellular logic, energy conservation principles, and physiological purpose without overwhelming chemical jargon.
- **Requirements:**
  - Clear explanations of *why* the pathway exists and what fuel problem it solves for the cell.
  - Step-by-step introduction of major substrates and end-products.
  - Accessible pathway schematics, cellular location tags (cytosol, mitochondrial matrix, inner membrane), and foundational checkpoints.
  - Explanations connecting fuel state (fed vs fasted) to cellular behavior.

### 2.2 Academic Tier (`academic`)
- **Target Audience:** Undergraduate medical, dental, pharmacy, nutrition, and biomedical science students.
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Rigorous intermediate metabolism: stoichiometric equations, key intermediate names, enzyme catalysts, cofactor requirements (NAD+, FAD, CoA, PLP, biotin, TPP), and regulatory mechanisms (allosteric feedback, substrate availability, covalent phosphorylation/dephosphorylation).
- **Requirements:**
  - Standardized biochemical nomenclature conforming to IUBMB enzyme classification.
  - Energetic accounting (ATP invested, ATP generated, reducing equivalents NADH/FADH2/NADPH).
  - Rate-limiting regulatory enzymes with specific allosteric effectors and hormonal regulation (insulin vs glucagon).
  - Transport mechanisms across cellular and mitochondrial membranes.
  - Organ-specific differences in enzyme expression (e.g., glucokinase vs hexokinase; glucose-6-phosphatase in liver vs absent in muscle).

### 2.3 Advanced & Research Tier (`advanced`)
- **Target Audience:** Senior medical students, biochemistry majors, endocrinology/metabolism fellows, clinical biochemists, and biomedical researchers.
- **Pedagogical Objective:** High-yield clinical correlations, inborn errors of metabolism, metabolic flux control analysis, genetic polymorphisms, pharmacological targets, and frontier research.
- **Requirements:**
  - Molecular pathophysiology of enzyme deficiencies (e.g., substrate accumulation, toxic bypass products, energy depletion).
  - Clinical biomarker interpretation (blood glucose, lactate/pyruvate ratio, ketone levels, ammonia, acylcarnitine profiles, urinary organic acids).
  - Pharmacological mechanisms of metabolic drugs (e.g., metformin/AMPK, statins/HMGCR, fibrates/PPAR-alpha, SGLT2 inhibitors).
  - Methodological considerations (stable isotope tracing, metabolomics, mass spectrometry, respirometry).
  - Citing primary literature and authoritative biochemical reference works.

## 3. Mandatory Chapter Structure

Each metabolism chapter content module must contain:

1. **Chapter Identification & Versioning:**
   - Stable curriculum ID (e.g., `metabolism.chapter.01`) and lesson ID (e.g., `topic.metabolism.intro-bioenergetics`).
   - Semantic version (integer $\ge 1$), ISO update date (`YYYY-MM-DD`), and explicit review status.
2. **Bilingual Metadata:**
   - Title and summary in fluent, idiomatic English and Bengali.
   - Learning objectives ($\ge 3$) articulating measurable cognitive competencies in both languages.
3. **Structured Content Sections (All Tiers):**
   - Minimum section paragraph length: 120 characters per core section in both languages.
   - Substantive explanations covering:
     - Overview & Biological Significance
     - Cellular Localization & Substrate Transport
     - Enzymatic Steps & Stoichiometry
     - Energetic Yield & Thermodynamics
     - Hormonal & Allosteric Regulation
     - Tissue Specialization & Crosstalk
     - Clinical Correlates & Pathophysiology
     - Key Terminology & Glossary
     - Summary & Takeaways
4. **Interactive & Visual Learning Elements:**
   - Interactive SVG metabolic pathway diagram with clickable enzyme/metabolite nodes.
   - Stepped exploratory pathway progression with keyboard and screen-reader accessibility.
   - Lab Mode compatibility (seamless switching between daylight and dark neon telemetry).
5. **Traceable Scientific References:**
   - Direct bibliographic attribution for every section.
   - Reputable textbook and journal sources with authors, title, organization, year, and URL/DOI.
6. **Formative Assessment:**
   - Peer-reviewed multiple-choice questions with clinically or biochemically grounded distractors and detailed bilingual rationales.

## 4. Reference Integrity & Authoritative Authorities

All biochemical assertions must be backed by reputable authorities:
- *Lehninger Principles of Biochemistry* (Nelson & Cox, Macmillan)
- *Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry* (Rodwell et al., McGraw-Hill)
- *Biochemistry* (Berg, Tymoczko, Gatto, Stryer, W.H. Freeman)
- *Marks' Basic Medical Biochemistry: A Clinical Approach* (Lieberman & Peet, Wolters Kluwer)
- *Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry* (Ferrier, Wolters Kluwer)
- *Medical Biochemistry* (Baynes & Dominiczak, Elsevier)
- *OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e / Biology 2e* (Rice University / OpenStax)
- *NCBI Bookshelf / StatPearls Clinical Biochemistry* (National Library of Medicine)
