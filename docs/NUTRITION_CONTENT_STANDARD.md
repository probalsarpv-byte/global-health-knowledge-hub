**Document Version:** 1.0.0  
**Status:** Authoritative Standard  
**Subject:** Functional Nutrition & Dietetics (`subject.nutrition`)  
**Target Platform:** Global Health Knowledge Hub  
**Permanent Academic Citation Rule:** Vancouver-Style Numeric Standard (`docs/ACADEMIC_WRITING_AND_CITATION_STANDARD.md`)

---

## 1. Educational Mission & Scope

The Functional Nutrition & Dietetics curriculum provides an evidence-based, scientifically rigorous, bilingual (English and Bengali) academic foundation in human nutrition, digestive physiology, cellular energetics, micronutrient biochemistry, gut microbiome biology, and medical nutrition therapy.

Every chapter must meet the highest standards of nutritional accuracy, pedagogical clarity, clinical safety, and accessibility.

---

## 2. Three-Tier Content Depth Architecture

Every chapter must implement three distinct depth tiers:

### 2.1 Foundation Tier (`foundation`)
- **Target Audience:** Introductory students, community health workers, allied health learners, and general science readers.
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Intuitive orientation, macroscopic context, core physiological purpose, and key dietary concepts without overwhelming molecular jargon.
- **Requirements:**
  - Clear explanations of *why* the nutrient or pathway matters to the human body.
  - Step-by-step introduction of major dietary sources, organ handling, and cellular functions.
  - Everyday intuitive analogies clearly distinguished from actual biological mechanisms.
  - High-yield takeaways and foundational checkpoints.

### 2.2 Academic Tier (`academic`)
- **Target Audience:** Undergraduate medical, nursing, dietetics, pharmacy, and biomedical science students.
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Rigorous physiological and biochemical mechanisms: enzymatic kinetics, apical/basolateral membrane transporters (e.g., SGLT1, GLUT5, PepT1, DMT1), endocrine feedback loops, and dietary reference intakes (RDA, AI, UL).
- **Requirements:**
  - Standardized anatomical, biochemical, and physiological nomenclature.
  - Energetic and stoichiometric accountability (e.g., ATP equivalents, respiratory quotients, grams of nitrogen per gram of protein).
  - Rate-limiting enzymes and receptor-mediated signaling pathways (e.g., GLP-1, leptin, FFAR2/3).
  - Tissue-specific metabolism (liver, enterocyte, skeletal muscle, adipose tissue, kidney).

### 2.3 Advanced & Research Tier (`advanced`)
- **Target Audience:** Clinical dietitians, physicians, endocrinologists, gastroenterologists, and nutritional scientists.
- **Pedagogical Objective:** Clinical trials synthesis, Medical Nutrition Therapy (MNT), pathophysiology of deficiency/toxicity/malabsorption, molecular nutrigenomics, and critical evaluation of clinical guidelines.
- **Requirements:**
  - Diagnostic criteria and evidence grading (e.g., GLIM criteria for malnutrition, ADA standards of care, ESPEN guidelines).
  - Quantitative clinical biomarker interpretation (e.g., HOMA-IR, serum ferritin, transferrin saturation, methylmalonic acid, fecal calprotectin).
  - Pharmacological and nutritional interactions (e.g., GLP-1 receptor agonists, statins, proton pump inhibitors and B12/iron absorption).
  - Primary literature citations and critical appraisal of methodology (e.g., randomized controlled feeding trials, Mendelian randomization).

## 3. Mandatory Vancouver Citation Standard Compliance

In strict compliance with `docs/ACADEMIC_WRITING_AND_CITATION_STANDARD.md`:
1. **Compact Numeric Format:** Use numeric citation markers `[1]`, `[2]`, `[1, 3]`.
2. **No Raw Bibliographic Text in Body:** Paragraphs must never contain raw URLs, publisher snippets, or citation lists.
3. **Sequential Numbering:** Sources are numbered by order of first appearance within the chapter.
4. **Source Reuse:** When a source is cited again in subsequent sections, its original reference number is reused.
5. **Bidirectional Navigation:** Inline citations link to `#ref-${chapterId}-${number}` with focus management, and reference items include accessible return links (`Return to citation [n]`).
6. **End-of-Chapter References:** Complete citation details (authors, title, publisher, year, URL) are placed strictly in the bibliography.

## 4. Bengali Language Quality (`বাংলা মানদণ্ড`)

- **Natural Scientific Prose:** Bengali text must read like authoritative, polished popular science and academic literature.
- **Bilingual Term Pairing:** Pair standard English terminology alongside Bengali terms on first introduction (e.g., *মোট দৈনিক শক্তি ব্যয় (Total Daily Energy Expenditure - TDEE)*, *ব্রাশ-বর্ডার হাইড্রোলাইসিস (Brush-border hydrolysis)*).
- **Depth Parity:** The Bengali content must match the English content in scientific depth, molecular nuance, and paragraph structure ($\ge 120$ characters per initial section paragraph).

---

## 5. Clinical Safety & Medical Disclaimers

1. **Educational Boundary:** All nutrition lessons are educational scientific syntheses and must state that they do not constitute individual medical diagnosis or prescriptive dietetic advice.
2. **Refusal to Generalize:** Acknowledge individual variability, age-dependent requirements, renal/hepatic impairment contraindications, and population-specific considerations.
3. **No Unsubstantiated Claims:** Strictly exclude fad diets, pseudoscientific assertions, unverified supplements, and therapeutic claims unsupported by high-quality clinical evidence.

## 6. Authoritative Reference Sources

All nutritional and biochemical assertions must draw from recognized authoritative references:
- *Modern Nutrition in Health and Disease* (Ross et al., Wolters Kluwer)
- *Krause and Mahan's Food & the Nutrition Care Process* (Raymond & Morrow, Elsevier)
- *Advanced Nutrition and Human Metabolism* (Gropper, Smith, Carr, Cengage)
- *Lehninger Principles of Biochemistry* (Nelson & Cox, Macmillan)
- *ESPEN Guidelines on Clinical Nutrition and Hydration*
- *World Health Organization (WHO) Nutrient Requirements and Dietary Guidelines*
- *Dietary Guidelines for Americans (USDA / HHS)*
- *National Institutes of Health (NIH) Office of Dietary Supplements*
